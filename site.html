Site Information – Natterjack @ Bath

Natterjack @ Bath is the primary outdoor learning site used by Be Wild Be Free. It is a mixed woodland and meadow habitat that offers rich opportunities for exploration, learning and play. The site is used under a landowner agreement and managed with care to ensure sustainability and minimal impact.

Overview of the Site

Natterjack @ Bath features a diverse landscape including:

  • mixed woodland with mature and young trees
  • a meadow/open field area for games and group activities
  • a designated fire circle
  • a tool-use area
  • a mud kitchen and sensory play zone
  • climbing trees and low-risk physical challenge opportunities
  • rope swings and balance-based play structures
  • wildlife habitats including fallen logs, deadwood and undergrowth
  • a compost/deadwood zone used for ecological learning
  • an access track and parking area

Boundaries

Site boundaries are agreed with the landowner and clearly communicated to children and adults at every session.

Boundary Rules

  • Children must remain within the designated Forest School area at all times.
  • New children are shown the perimeter during the introductory walk.
  • Markers, natural landmarks or rope boundaries may be used.
  • Adults position themselves strategically to supervise boundary edges.

The boundary is introduced with child-friendly language such as: “The boundary keeps us safe — just like a big hug around our Forest School.”

Access & Parking

The site is accessed via a track used occasionally by landowner vehicles. Children are briefed to stop and stand still if a vehicle is seen or heard.

  • Parking is limited; arrangements vary by group.
  • Drop-off and pick-up points are communicated in advance.
  • Children walk as a supervised group to the main woodland area.

Site Features & Learning Opportunities

Woodland

The woodland provides:

  • shelter from sun, wind and light rain
  • natural resources for play and crafts
  • habitat exploration opportunities
  • tree climbing and balance challenges

Meadow / Open Field

  • Space for team games and large-group activities
  • Wildflowers and seasonal plant identification
  • Open-sky activities such as cloud reading or star-gazing (winter)

Fire Circle

  • A designated area used only under adult supervision
  • Clear fire triangle and seating positions
  • Used for cooking, storytelling and group reflection

Tool Area

  • Designated safe zone for saws, knives, loppers and drills
  • Tools used on 1:1 or 1:2 supervision ratio

Mud Kitchen

  • Open-ended play using soil, water and natural materials
  • Great for sensory regulation and imaginative play

Rope Swings & Climbing Trees

  • Risk-assessed natural movement opportunities
  • Daily checks completed on ropes, branches and anchors

No Indoor Fallback Space

Natterjack @ Bath does not include an indoor facility. Sessions proceed outdoors in most weather conditions with the exception of:

  • high winds exceeding 38mph
  • thunderstorms
  • extreme temperatures where safety cannot be maintained

Backup plans may include shelter building, tarp use, shorter sessions, or session rescheduling.

Hazards Specific to the Site

Trees & Deadwood

  • Daily visual check for hanging branches
  • Climbing restricted to safe trees only

Ground Conditions

  • May become slippery after rain
  • Natural trip hazards: roots, rocks, uneven surfaces

Seasonal Hazards

  • Nettles, brambles and thorny undergrowth
  • Ticks (summer and early autumn)
  • Wasps and bees during warm months

Wildlife

  • Children taught respectful wildlife behaviour
  • “No pick, no lick, no squish” rule enforced

Emergency Procedures

Muster Point

A safe, clear gathering area is used during emergencies. Children are trained to go there when asked.

Emergency Equipment

  • Fully stocked forest school first aid kit
  • Emergency contact information
  • Mobile phone
  • Fire blanket and extinguishing supplies

Evacuation

If evacuation is needed due to weather, fire risk or incident, children walk as a group with staff to the designated pickup point.

Environmental Considerations

The site is rich in biodiversity. We operate with care to ensure:

  • habitat protection
  • responsible use of natural resources
  • minimal ground impact
  • careful management of fire and tool zones

Children learn how to care for the land through practical involvement in conservation and seasonal observation.

Seasonal Considerations

Spring

  • New plant growth—avoid trampling sensitive areas
  • Increased insect activity

Summer

  • Hydration and sun safety required
  • Higher risk of wasps/ticks

Autumn

  • Slippery leaf cover
  • Acorns, nuts and fungi present

Winter

  • Cold weather gear essential
  • Potential icy patches

Site Responsibilities

  • Children learn to care for nature through leave-no-trace practices.
  • Staff ensure sessions respect landowner agreements and ecological balance.
  • Boundary checks and pre-session walk-throughs ensure safety and suitability.

Summary

Natterjack @ Bath is a beautiful, varied and inspiring site that provides endless opportunities for nature-based learning. Through careful planning, risk assessment and environmental stewardship, Be Wild Be Free ensures that this landscape remains safe, sustainable and magical for every child who visits.
