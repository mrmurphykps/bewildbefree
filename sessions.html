Forest School Sessions

At Be Wild Be Free, our sessions follow the Forest School ethos of long-term, child-led, experiential learning in the natural environment. While every session responds flexibly to the children and the season, there is a familiar rhythm that helps children feel safe, confident and grounded.

Session Rhythm & Structure

1. Arrival & Welcome

  • Children are welcomed at the meeting point by staff.
  • Register is taken and medical information is reviewed.
  • Children walk as a group to the Forest School site.
  • Session boundaries and safety reminders are shared.

2. Circle Time

  • A short grounding activity to settle into the space.
  • Check-in: feelings, needs, ideas, themes.
  • Introduce any planned invitations or seasonal opportunities.

3. Child-Led Exploration

This is the heart of Forest School. Children choose from a range of natural opportunities:

  • tree climbing
  • exploring woodland and meadow areas
  • nature crafts & tool use
  • den building
  • rope swings & balance challenges
  • mud kitchen play
  • tracking wildlife & observing nature
  • fire-lighting (when appropriate)

Practitioners support learning through observation, conversation, modelling skills and providing well-timed challenges.

4. Group Activities (Optional)

Some sessions may include optional guided experiences:

  • fire circle gatherings
  • storytelling or song
  • seasonal crafts (e.g., willow weaving)
  • campfire cooking
  • team challenges

5. Reflection & Closing Circle

  • Share discoveries, feelings, achievements.
  • Celebrate resilience, kindness and creativity.
  • Pack away resources and restore the site (“leave no trace”).
  • Walk together to the pick-up point.

Child-Led Learning

Forest School is child-led. This means:

  • children choose their own activities
  • adults observe more than direct
  • learning emerges naturally through play and curiosity
  • adults support children’s ideas with resources, safety guidance and encouragement

This approach builds:

  • self-esteem and independence
  • problem-solving and perseverance
  • creativity and critical thinking
  • collaboration and communication

Clothing & Kit List

Forest School sessions run in all weathers except dangerous conditions. To stay comfortable and safe, children must wear:

  • Long sleeves and long trousers (all seasons)
  • Waterproof coat and trousers
  • Warm layers in winter (fleece, thermals)
  • Hat and gloves (winter)
  • Sun hat and sunscreen (summer)
  • Wellies or sturdy boots
  • Spare socks

If clothing is unsuitable for safety or comfort, we may adapt activities or shorten sessions.

Fire & Tool Use in Sessions

Fire

  • Only trained staff maintain a lit fire.
  • Children learn safe approaches and behaviour.
  • Fire is managed in a designated fire circle.
  • Water buckets and fire blanket always present.

Tools

  • Tools used only under 1:1 or 1:2 supervision.
  • Child-friendly and size-appropriate options.
  • Tools cleaned, checked and stored safely.
  • Children taught safe working positions and respect for tools.

Tools and fire are powerful learning opportunities that build confidence, responsibility and awareness.

Group Agreements with Children

We co-create simple rules with children to keep everyone safe:

  • Look after yourself
  • Look after each other
  • Look after the environment

These principles guide behaviour and support a positive learning climate.

Supporting Additional Needs (SEND)

Outdoor learning is naturally supportive of many SEND profiles. We work closely with parents and schools to understand individual needs and adapt sessions accordingly.

Support strategies may include:

  • visuals & clear routines
  • safety buddies
  • quieter areas for regulation
  • movement & proprioceptive activities
  • individual risk assessments
  • adjusted tasks or tools

Communication with Parents & Carers

We value strong relationships with families.

Before the programme:

  • Parents receive kit list, location info, consent forms and key policies.

During the programme:

  • Updates may be shared through email or school liaison.
  • Any behavioural or wellbeing concerns addressed early.
  • Accidents/injuries communicated the same day.

End of programme:

  • Celebration of children’s achievements.
  • Summary of learning and progress.

Arrival & Collection Procedures

Arrival

  • Children arrive at the designated pick-up point.
  • Register taken and gear checked.
  • Walk to site as a supervised group.

Collection

  • Children return to the same pick-up point.
  • Dismissed only to known adults or authorised contacts.

Late collection is managed according to safeguarding procedures.

What Children Learn at Forest School

Forest School develops the whole child:

  • resilience and emotional regulation
  • teamwork & communication
  • creativity, imagination & problem-solving
  • physical strength, balance & coordination
  • risk awareness and decision-making
  • environmental understanding & care

Children leave sessions feeling more capable, more connected, and more confident.
