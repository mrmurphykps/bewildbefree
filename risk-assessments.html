Risk Assessments

Be Wild Be Free uses comprehensive risk–benefit assessments (RBAs) for all activities, tools, fire use, weather, and site-specific hazards. These assessments follow Health & Safety Executive (HSE) guidance and the Forest School Association (FSA) best practice framework. Risk–benefit thinking recognises that managed risk supports confidence, resilience, and independence.

Our RBA Method

We follow this approach for all assessments:

  1. Identify benefits – developmental, physical, emotional, social & ecological learning gains.
  2. Identify hazards – anything with the potential to cause harm.
  3. Assess risk – likelihood + severity.
  4. Control measures – actions to reduce risk to an acceptable level.
  5. Evaluate residual risk – checking safety remains appropriate.
  6. Review regularly – yearly or after incidents/changes.

The following sections outline our main risk–benefit assessments for Forest School practice at Natterjack @ Bath.

1. Site Risk Assessment – Natterjack @ Bath

Natterjack @ Bath is a mixed woodland and meadow environment with natural features including trees, uneven ground, wildlife habitats, rope swings, climbing trees, a fire circle, mud kitchen area, and compost/deadwood zones. There is no indoor fallback space.

Key Hazards

  • Uneven terrain, slippery mud, loose stone pathways
  • Low branches, falling deadwood, natural debris
  • Seasonal hazards: nettles, brambles, ticks
  • Weather exposure (cold, heat, high wind)
  • Nearby access track used for vehicles
  • Mud/water areas, puddles, ground saturation
  • Wildlife (wasps, stings, bites, droppings)

Control Measures

  • Daily site check before every session
  • Tree safety visual assessment (deadwood, wind movement)
  • Boundaries clearly explained to children
  • Children supervised closely near uneven ground
  • Group first aid kit carried at all times
  • Clear muster point for emergencies
  • Weather monitored using Met Office forecast

2. Daily Site Check

Completed before every session:

  • Check for new hazards (fallen branches, debris, litter)
  • Inspect fire circle area
  • Ensure tools are safe & maintained
  • Check for animal activity (wasp nests, droppings)
  • Inspect rope swings for knots, fraying, anchor stability
  • Check mud kitchen & tool area for hazards
  • Review weather conditions, wind speed, temperature
  • Ensure first aid kit and emergency equipment is present

3. Weather Risk Assessment

Hazards

  • High winds – risk of falling branches
  • Electrical storms – lightning risk
  • Extreme cold – hypothermia risk
  • Heat – dehydration, heat exhaustion

Control Measures

  • No sessions in winds above 38 mph
  • Move group away from trees if wind increases
  • Cancel/postpone during thunder & lightning
  • Winter: ensure warm layers, hats, gloves
  • Summer: ensure shade, water breaks, sunscreen
  • Continuous dynamic risk assessment throughout the session

4. Fire Circle RBA

Benefits

  • Resilience, teamwork, responsibility
  • Cooking skills & life skills
  • Respect for fire and heat

Hazards

  • Burns from embers or flames
  • Smoke inhalation
  • Tripping or falling into fire circle

Controls

  • Established fire triangle perimeter
  • Children taught “respect position” (one knee down)
  • Water buckets and fire blanket present
  • No running near the fire
  • Only staff manage active fire
  • Long hair tied back; no loose clothing

5. Tool Use RBA

Tools used may include bow saws, knives, loppers, mallets, peelers, and palm drills.

Benefits

  • Fine motor development
  • Confidence & independence
  • Practical life skills
  • Understanding risk & safe practice

General Controls

  • 1:1 or 1:2 supervision during tool use
  • Designated “tool area” with clear boundaries
  • Tools kept sharp, clean, and safe
  • Correct stance demonstrated (e.g., blood bubble awareness)
  • PPE used as appropriate

Bow Saw

  • Used seated or kneeling
  • Second adult stabilises material if needed
  • Blade guard in place when not in use

Fixed Blade Knife / Whittling

  • Use in “safety seat” position
  • Cut away from body and others
  • Workpiece braced securely

Loppers

  • Only cut materials appropriate to tool size
  • Hands kept away from blades

Palm Drill

  • Used on stable surfaces only
  • Ensure no hand behind drilling point

6. Rope Swings RBA

Hazards

  • Falls
  • Entanglement
  • Collision

Controls

  • Anchor point inspected daily
  • Drop zone cleared & safe
  • One child on swing at a time
  • Staff spot for younger children

7. Climbing Trees RBA

Hazards

  • Branch failure
  • Falls
  • Scrapes & splinters

Controls

  • Tree inspected for rot or deadwood
  • Climbing height: no higher than adult’s shoulder
  • Staff spot; ground cleared for landing zone

8. Mud Kitchen & Soil Play RBA

Hazards

  • Slips, trips and falls
  • Hygiene concerns
  • Loose water on surfaces

Controls

  • Handwashing before food
  • Non-slip footwear
  • Mud kitchen equipment checked

9. Wildlife & Flora RBA

Hazards

  • Stings, bites, ticks
  • Toxic plants (e.g., foxglove)
  • Animal droppings

Controls

  • Teach “no pick, no lick, no squish” rule
  • Long sleeves/long trousers recommended
  • Tick checks in summer
  • Adults handle plant ID & wildlife movement

10. Water, Ground & Terrain RBA

Hazards

  • Slippery mud
  • Puddles & runoff channels
  • Roots, rocks, uneven surfaces

Controls

  • Staff supervise movement in tricky terrain
  • Children taught safe walking techniques
  • Sturdy footwear encouraged

11. Emergency Procedure RBA

Hazards

  • Lost child
  • Medical emergency
  • Fire emergency

Controls

  • 1:6 ratio or better
  • Emergency plan briefed every session
  • Muster point pre-agreed
  • Emergency phone & contact list carried
  • Clear protocols for calling emergency services

Review Schedule

  • All RAs reviewed annually
  • Reviews occur after incidents or changes
  • Dynamic RA completed continuously
