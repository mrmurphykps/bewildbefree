Ecology & Sustainability

Be Wild Be Free operates with a deep respect for the natural world. We view ourselves not just as users of the land, but as caretakers and partners in its wellbeing. Every session is designed to nurture children’s connection to the environment and to foster an understanding of their role in protecting it.

Our Environmental Philosophy

Children who spend regular time outdoors develop empathy for living things, a sense of environmental responsibility, and a long-lasting bond with nature. We aim to create experiences that inspire children to care for the land—not because they are told to, but because they feel it.

Our ecological approach is guided by three principles:

  • Respect – for wildlife, habitats, plants and natural cycles.
  • Responsibility – our actions should leave the site healthy and thriving.
  • Reciprocity – we give back to the land through stewardship and mindful use.

Leave No Trace

We follow Leave No Trace principles adapted for Forest School and child-led learning:

  • We leave the site as we found it — or better.
  • We minimise disturbance to plants and animals.
  • We take all litter home, even if we didn’t create it.
  • We avoid unnecessary picking or damaging of living plants.
  • We use natural materials responsibly and sparingly.
  • We encourage children to respect habitats (logs, burrows, nests).

Children learn these values through experience, modelling, stories and practical conservation tasks.

Wildlife Protection

Natterjack @ Bath is home to a wide range of species—birds, insects, mammals, fungi, and plants. Wildlife safety is embedded in every session.

  • We teach “No pick, no lick, no squish” for plants and creatures.
  • We observe animals at a distance.
  • Children learn to listen for birds, search for tracks, and spot habitats.
  • We avoid disturbing nests, burrows and roosting areas.
  • We check for seasonal activity (frogs, birds, insects) before setting up zones.

Deadwood & Compost Zones

Deadwood areas are some of the most biodiverse microhabitats in the woodland. We maintain and monitor:

  • Deadwood piles – home to insects, fungi, small mammals and decomposers.
  • Compost areas – used for natural decomposition demonstrations.
  • Leaf litter habitats – perfect for bug hunts and seasonal studies.

Children learn the importance of decomposition, nutrient cycles, and woodland regeneration.

Seasonal Ecological Learning

Each season brings new learning opportunities:

Spring

  • Buds, bird nests, wildflower growth
  • Understanding pollinators
  • Respectful observation of frogspawn (if present)

Summer

  • Insect life cycles
  • Tree identification through leaves
  • Meadow pollinator activity

Autumn

  • Fungi exploration (no touching unless guided)
  • Changing leaves and seed dispersal
  • Foraging education (non-picking unless planned)

Winter

  • Animal tracking
  • Frost patterns and hibernation
  • Tree identification by bark and branching

Seasons help children understand natural cycles and deepen their connection to the land.

Respecting Plant Life

We actively teach children to care for plants through:

  • knowing which plants are fragile or protected
  • avoiding trampling areas with young shoots
  • only taking natural materials that are abundant and already fallen
  • learning to identify common UK woodland plants
  • understanding simple plant ecology and photosynthesis concepts

Any plant harvesting (for crafts or cooking) is minimal, purposeful and supervised.

Campfires & Environmental Impact

Campfires are used thoughtfully, following:

  • Forest School Association good practice
  • Local landowner guidelines
  • Sustainable firewood collection principles

We only burn:

  • dry, seasoned wood brought to site, OR
  • small amounts of fallen deadwood that do not harm habitats

Fires are fully extinguished with water and dispersed safely, leaving no charred remains.

Waste & Materials

Be Wild Be Free minimises waste by:

  • reusing natural materials
  • avoiding single-use plastics
  • carrying out all waste, even if not ours
  • using biodegradable products where possible
  • using natural dyes, charcoal, clay and mud for crafts

We encourage children to see waste as a shared responsibility.

Sustainable Practice with Tools

Tools are used in a way that supports sustainability:

  • Only dead, fallen wood is cut unless part of conservation work.
  • Children are taught correct cutting techniques.
  • Projects are purposeful and not wasteful.
  • Wood shavings are returned to natural decomposition cycles.

Teaching mindful resource use helps children develop lifelong environmental responsibility.

Ecological Impact & Monitoring

We regularly review our impact on the land through:

  • seasonal site assessments
  • footfall and ground-wear monitoring
  • hazard and habitat checks
  • landowner feedback

If an area shows signs of overuse, we rotate zones or pause activities to allow recovery.

Children as Stewards

A core part of our practice is empowering children to care for nature. This includes:

  • simple conservation tasks
  • woodland tidy routines
  • bug habitat creation
  • watering and caring for plants (where appropriate)
  • litter picking (only when safe)

Children learn that their actions matter and that they have a positive role in protecting the natural world.

Summary

Our ecological approach is woven through everything we do. We want children to leave our sessions not only with practical skills, but also with a deep, instinctive love for nature — one that inspires respect, responsibility and environmental stewardship for life.
